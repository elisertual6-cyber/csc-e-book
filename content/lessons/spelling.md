# Compare carefully instead of guessing from appearance

Draft editorial sample · Subprofessional track · Version 0.1

This original teaching draft awaits independent correctness, originality and language review. Spelling examples target conventional edited English. Regional variants and actual clerical procedures require their own stated standards; official CSC coverage remains unverified.

## Learning objectives

- Check conventional spellings without relying on visual familiarity alone.
- Distinguish spelling correction from exact transcription.
- Detect substitutions, omissions and transposed characters in short records.

## Explanation

Some misspellings look familiar because they preserve a word's overall shape. Slow down at repeated letters and unstressed syllables. For example, accommodation includes double c and double m; separate has a rather than e in its middle syllable. These observations describe particular words, not universal rules that generate every English spelling.

Use an appropriate dictionary or an approved organizational reference when the task permits it. English has regional alternatives, so an item should avoid treating two accepted variants as though only one could be correct without specifying the target convention. An automated spelling suggestion is not independent editorial approval.

Exact transcription is a different task. If the source is an identifier, preserve its characters rather than silently correcting what looks unusual. A zero and a capital O may look similar, but they are different characters. A swapped digit can change a record even if the result remains a plausible number.

Check in small groups, then perform a full comparison. Verify the prefix, each digit and the ending. In a real workflow, follow its privacy and verification rules rather than exposing customer records to public tools. The invented identifiers here contain no personal records.

## Worked example

Source: **Q-5831**. Copies: Q-5831, Q-5381 and Q-5837.

Only **Q-5831** is exact. Q-5381 transposes the middle digits, while Q-5837 changes the last digit. The question is copying accuracy, so deciding which identifier looks more usual would be irrelevant.

For a spelling task, compare **necessary** with neccessary. The conventional spelling necessary has one c and two s letters. Check the actual letter sequence rather than the approximate visual shape.

## Practice

1. Check the spelling of receive and recieve against the target convention.
2. Compare source ID T-6409 with copy T-6049. Describe the error if there is one.
3. Explain why an unfamiliar identifier should not be silently replaced by a more familiar word during exact transcription.

## Rationale

For item 1, receive is the conventional spelling. This individual example does not make “i before e” an exception-free rule. For item 2, the middle digits are transposed. For item 3, the source characters define the copying task; a silent substitution would change the record rather than reproduce it.

## Reflection

State whether the task is spelling, ordering or exact copying before answering. Independent review should verify word forms with a suitable reference and compare every character in accuracy examples.
