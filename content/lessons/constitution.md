# Read constitutional principles with their article references

Draft editorial sample · Common track · Version 0.1

This original, ungraded teaching draft awaits independent correctness, originality, source and language review. It is not an official CSC lesson or a claim of verified 2027 coverage. Locate basic constitutional allocations and rights without inventing case-specific rules.

## Learning objectives

- Identify the constitutional text associated with a stated principle.
- Distinguish legislative, executive and judicial allocations.
- Separate a basic textual identification from a legal conclusion about a particular case.

## Explanation

A constitutional study note should state the proposition and the article or section used to support it. Article II, Section 1 of the 1987 Constitution identifies the Philippines as a democratic and republican State and places sovereignty in the people. Article XI, Section 1 states that public office is a public trust. These principles are related, but they appear in different places and answer different questions.

For the allocation of powers, Article VI, Section 1 addresses legislative power in Congress, with its reservation concerning initiative and referendum. Article VII, Section 1 vests executive power in the President. Article VIII, Section 1 addresses judicial power in one Supreme Court and lower courts established by law. A simple branch-identification exercise does not settle all checks, exceptions or disputed applications.

The Bill of Rights in Article III includes due process and equal protection in Section 1, unreasonable searches and seizures in Section 2, and speech, expression, press, peaceable assembly and petition in Section 4. Avoid turning a short summary into an absolute rule such as “every search always requires the same procedure” or “a freedom has no possible lawful limitations.” Case law and specific facts require separate qualified legal review.

## Worked example

Question: Which institution is named in Article VII, Section 1 as the holder of executive power? Answer: the President of the Philippines. The method is textual identification. It is not an inference that every act labelled executive is automatically valid or immune from review.

## Practice

1. Match legislative, executive and judicial power with the constitutional bodies named above.
2. Locate the article containing “public office is a public trust.”
3. Explain why identifying the search-and-seizure provision does not decide whether a particular search was lawful.

## Rationale

For 1, the basic allocations are Congress, the President, and the Supreme Court plus lower courts established by law, with the stated constitutional qualifications. For 2, consult Article XI, Section 1. For 3, legality can depend on facts, recognised exceptions, applicable laws and jurisprudence beyond the short identification exercise.

## Reflection

Keep a reference beside each proposition. Mark uncertain applications for qualified review instead of choosing an answer solely because it sounds familiar.
