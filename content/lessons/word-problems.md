# Turn the story into a model

Draft editorial lesson · Common curriculum · Version 0.1

Original teaching material awaiting independent review. All quantities and scenarios are illustrative; these exercises are ungraded and make no official CSC claims.

## Learning objectives

- Separate known quantities, unknown quantities and assumptions.
- Use units to connect distance, time, cost and work rates.
- Check an equation and its result against the original situation.

## Explanation

Start by identifying what the question asks for. Write down the known quantities with their units and define an unknown. Then describe the relationship in words before choosing operations. A total cost might be “fixed fee plus unit cost times number of units”; a journey at constant speed uses “distance equals speed times time.”

Units are part of the model. If speed is in kilometres per hour, time must be in hours when using distance = speed × time. Thirty minutes is 0.5 hour, not 0.30 hour. If printing costs pesos per page, multiplying by pages gives pesos; adding that result to a page count would not make sense.

For work done at constant independent rates, use work per unit of time. A machine completing one job in 6 hours works at 1/6 job per hour. If another completes the same-sized job in 3 hours, their combined rate is 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 job per hour. Under those assumptions, one job takes 1 ÷ (1/2) = 2 hours. Adding their completion times would model a different situation.

Check assumptions such as constant speed, no breaks, identical jobs or a fixed price. A mathematically correct calculation can still be inappropriate if the situation does not satisfy the model.

## Worked example

A printing service charges a fixed preparation fee of ₱30 plus ₱2 for each page. A learner has exactly ₱150 for this job. How many pages can be printed, assuming no other fees?

Let p be the number of pages. The cost model is **30 + 2p = 150**.

1. Subtract the fixed fee: 2p = 120.
2. Divide by the per-page charge: p = **60 pages**.
3. Check: ₱30 + (₱2/page × 60 pages) = ₱150.

Dividing the full ₱150 by ₱2 would forget the preparation fee and produce an unaffordable answer.

## Practice

Write an equation with units, solve it and substitute the result back into the story.

1. A bicycle travels at a constant 12 kilometres per hour for 45 minutes without stopping. How far does it travel?
2. Four identical notebooks and a ₱20 delivery charge cost ₱180 in total. What is the price of one notebook?
3. One machine completes a job in 4 hours and another completes the same-sized job in 12 hours. Working together at constant independent rates, how long do they take?

## Rationale

1. **9 kilometres.** Convert 45 minutes to 45/60 = 0.75 hour. Distance = 12 km/hour × 0.75 hour = 9 km. The result is less than the 12 km travelled in a full hour.
2. **₱40 per notebook.** Let n be the unit price in pesos. Then 4n + 20 = 180, so 4n = 160 and n = 40. Check: 4 × ₱40 + ₱20 = ₱180.
3. **3 hours.** Their combined rate is 1/4 + 1/12 = 3/12 + 1/12 = 1/3 job per hour. One job therefore takes 3 hours, which is shorter than either machine's individual completion time.

## Reflection

Before accepting an answer, ask whether its unit matches the question, whether its size is reasonable, and whether it satisfies the original equation. Write any assumptions explicitly instead of silently adding information.
