# Name the relationship before matching the words

Draft editorial sample · Professional track · Version 0.1

This original teaching draft awaits independent correctness, originality and language review. Track placement is provisional and does not establish official CSC coverage. It is an ungraded preview, not an approved assessment.

## Learning objectives

- Express the relationship between two words as a specific sentence.
- Preserve the direction and type of that relationship in a second pair.
- Reject associations that do not satisfy the stated relationship.

## Explanation

An analogy compares relationships, not just familiar words. In “thermometer : temperature,” a useful relationship is “the instrument measures the quantity.” That is more precise than “both words concern science.” A matching pair should support the same sentence in the same direction.

Common practice relationships include part to whole, tool to function, worker to product and an ordered pair of opposites. A single pair can support several descriptions. When an exercise names a relationship explicitly, use that requirement. When it does not, test whether the options make one relationship clearly preferable. If two answers fit equally well, the item needs revision.

Direction matters. “A wheel is part of a bicycle” does not match a pair that says a whole contains a part in the reverse order. Similarly, “a tool measures a quantity” differs from “a tool cuts a material,” even though both are tool-related.

Do not force a match through an obscure fact or a private association. A defensible answer should use a shared, relevant meaning and explain why the other options fail. Familiarity with a topic alone is not evidence that its words preserve the relationship.

## Worked example

Use the relationship **instrument measures quantity**: thermometer is to temperature as weighing scale is to what?

**Mass** fits the intended measurement relationship in this exercise. A scale is not related to sound, time or distance through that same measurement role. This example uses a scale intended to report mass; actual instruments and displayed units should always be specified when a technical distinction could affect an answer.

## Practice

1. Under a part-to-whole relationship, complete: wheel is to bicycle as page is to what?
2. Under a tool-to-function relationship, complete: scissors are to cutting as a broom is to what?
3. Explain why “bicycle : wheel” reverses the direction in item 1.

## Rationale

For item 1, book gives the intended relation: a page is part of a book. For item 2, sweeping gives the intended function. Item 3 puts the whole first and the part second, unlike wheel to bicycle. These supplied relationships narrow the task; without adequate context, a reviewer should consider whether other pairings could also be defended.

## Reflection

Write one sentence that describes both pairs before choosing an answer. Independent review should test all distractors under the same relationship and check any technical vocabulary used in an item.
