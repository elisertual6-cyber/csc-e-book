# Connect ideas without losing the structure

Draft editorial sample · Common curriculum · Version 0.1

This original teaching draft awaits independent correctness, originality and language review. It teaches formal edited English through original examples, not confidential or official examination material.

## Learning objectives

- Match connecting words to contrast, cause and consequence.
- Keep items in a list grammatically parallel.
- Place introductory modifiers beside the person or thing they describe.
- Join independent clauses without a comma splice or fused sentence.

## Explanation

First identify the relationship between ideas. Because introduces a reason; although signals a contrast or concession; therefore introduces a conclusion or consequence. These words have different grammatical jobs. A suitable meaning does not make every connector interchangeable in every sentence position.

Compare “We paused because the printer stopped” with “The printer stopped; therefore, we paused.” In the second example, therefore is a transitional adverb. A comma by itself is not the usual way to join the two independent clauses in formal prose. A period, a semicolon or an appropriate coordinating conjunction can solve the boundary problem.

Parallel lists give items matching grammatical forms. “The task is to record, verify and label” uses three base-form verbs. Mixing “record, verifying and labeled” disrupts that structure. The correct form depends on the sentence's opening, so read the whole list.

Pronouns also need clear references. In “Both clerks said they were ready,” they refers to the two clerks. A reader should not have to guess which person or group a pronoun represents. Check the stated antecedent rather than choosing a pronoun in isolation.

An introductory modifier should clearly attach to its intended subject. “After checking the totals, the clerk signed the report” makes the clerk the person checking. “After checking the totals, the report was signed” leaves that actor unstated and creates a dangling modifier in the formal convention taught here. Revise to make the intended actor explicit.

## Worked example

Complete: “The checklist asks staff to record the date, verify the amount and ___ the folder.” Options include label, labeling and labeled.

**Label** matches record and verify. All three actions depend on “asks staff to.” The shared to need not be repeated before every verb. Select the form that completes the existing structure rather than inventing an entirely different sentence.

## Practice

1. Fill in a contrast connector: “The team continued along the covered walkway, ___ it was raining.” Choose although or because to express a concession.
2. Repair the boundary: “The list was complete, the clerk filed it.” Give two valid versions.
3. Repair the modifier: “After reading the instructions, the form was completed by the applicant.” State clearly who read the instructions.

## Rationale

For item 1, although expresses the requested concession: the activity continued despite the rain. Because would instead present rain as the reason. For item 2, use “The list was complete; the clerk filed it” or “The list was complete, and the clerk filed it.” A full stop is another possible boundary. A connector must preserve the intended relationship.

For item 3, “After reading the instructions, the applicant completed the form” places the applicant beside the introductory phrase. The point is clarity about the actor, not a blanket rule that passive voice is always wrong.

## Reflection

Check meaning, structure and punctuation separately. A fluent-sounding option can still express the wrong relationship. During independent review, test every distractor and record why exactly one answer meets the task's stated requirement.
