# Different forms, the same amount

Draft editorial lesson · Common curriculum · Version 0.1

Original teaching material awaiting independent review. These examples are ungraded learning activities, not actual CSC questions or verified 2027 specifications.

## Learning objectives

- Express one amount as a fraction, decimal and percentage.
- Find a fraction or percentage of a stated whole.
- Distinguish a percentage change from a change in percentage points.

## Explanation

A fraction compares a number of equal parts with the number of parts in one whole. In 3/8, the numerator 3 counts the selected parts and the denominator 8 describes the equal division. The denominator cannot be zero. Equivalent fractions represent the same value: multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number preserves the value.

Divide the numerator by the denominator to convert a fraction to a decimal. To express a decimal as a percentage, multiply by 100 and add the percent sign. Thus **3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%**. Conversely, 12% means 12/100, or 0.12. A percentage needs a clear base: 12% of 50 and 12% of 200 are different amounts.

To add fractions, first express them using a common denominator. For example, 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6; adding the denominators would change the size of the parts. To find a fraction of a quantity, multiply the quantity by the fraction.

For a percentage increase, divide the increase by the original amount and multiply by 100%. A rise from 40% to 50% is **10 percentage points**, but the relative increase is (50 − 40)/40 = 25%. Say which comparison you mean.

## Worked example

A study group budgets ₱800 for printing. A shop offers a 15% discount on the entire printing bill, with no other fees. Find the discount and the amount paid.

1. Convert 15% to 0.15.
2. Multiply ₱800 × 0.15 = **₱120 discount**.
3. Subtract ₱120 from ₱800: **₱680 paid**.

Check by finding 85% of the original bill: ₱800 × 0.85 = ₱680. The discount is part of the original ₱800, not part of the discounted price.

## Practice

Work on paper before reading the rationale. Include the whole or base in each answer.

1. A box contains 64 study cards. Three-eighths are vocabulary cards. How many vocabulary cards are there?
2. A reading target increases from 80 pages to 100 pages. What is the percentage increase?
3. A class completion rate changes from 60% to 75%. State the change in percentage points and the relative percentage increase.

## Rationale

1. **24 cards.** Compute 64 × 3/8 = 8 × 3 = 24. As a reverse check, 24/64 simplifies to 3/8.
2. **25% increase.** The increase is 20 pages; the original base is 80 pages. Therefore 20/80 × 100% = 25%. Dividing by the new target would answer a different question.
3. **15 percentage points; 25% relative increase.** The difference between the rates is 75 − 60 = 15 points. Relative to the original rate, 15/60 × 100% = 25%.

## Reflection

Before calculating a percentage, write: “The whole or original amount is …”. If the question concerns a change, check whether it asks for an amount, a percentage change or percentage points. These are different units of comparison.
