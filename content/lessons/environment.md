# Prevent waste and compare environmental claims carefully

Draft editorial sample · Common track · Version 0.1

This original, ungraded teaching draft awaits independent correctness, originality, source and language review. It is not an official CSC lesson or a claim of verified 2027 coverage. Distinguish reduction, reuse, appropriate recovery and evidence-based resource comparisons.

## Learning objectives

- Distinguish preventing waste from reusing and processing materials.
- Identify source segregation and the open-burning prohibition as referenced waste-management topics.
- Compare energy quantities using both power and operating time.

## Explanation

Reduction prevents unnecessary waste, for example choosing less unnecessary packaging before purchase. Reuse gives an existing item another appropriate use. Recycling processes suitable materials into recovered material; composting handles suitable biodegradable organic matter through an appropriate process. The categories describe different actions, not interchangeable labels for anything considered environmentally friendly.

RA 9003, Section 21, addresses segregation primarily at source. Keep materials separated according to the applicable collection and handling instructions rather than assuming that mixing them first will be harmless. Section 48 includes open burning of solid waste among prohibited acts. Used batteries and other special wastes need the designated collection or authorised handling route, not a food-scrap compost pile. Real local arrangements and legal requirements must be checked.

Compare quantities on a common basis. Electrical energy is power multiplied by time: two lamps providing equal useful light over the same period can use different energy. A leaking tap wastes water continuously until the physical problem is repaired; reminders do not themselves fix a leak. Vegetation can help reduce erosion, but it does not make every slope immune to landslides. Be cautious about absolute environmental claims unsupported by the stated evidence.

## Worked example

At equal light output for five hours, an 8-watt lamp uses 8 × 5 = 40 watt-hours, while a 12-watt lamp uses 60 watt-hours. The first uses 20 watt-hours less in this comparison. This is not a complete product lifecycle assessment and does not account for other performance or disposal factors.

## Practice

1. Is choosing less packaging before purchase reduction or reuse?
2. Explain why vegetable peelings and used batteries should not be sent into the same compost stream.
3. Calculate the energy used by a 10-watt device running for three hours.

## Rationale

For 1, reduction, because waste is avoided before being created. For 2, peelings may be suitable compost feedstock whereas batteries require appropriate special-waste handling; follow actual local instructions. For 3, 10 × 3 = 30 watt-hours, or 0.03 kilowatt-hour.

## Reflection

Check both the factual claim and its comparison base. A useful action should not be presented as a guarantee that every environmental risk disappears.
