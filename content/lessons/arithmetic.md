# A little order goes a long way

Draft editorial sample · Common curriculum · Version 0.1

This original sample illustrates the lesson format. It has not received independent editorial approval and is not a published exam item.

## Learning objectives

- Recognize the order of arithmetic operations.
- Translate a short everyday situation into an expression.
- Check a result using a second method.

## Explanation

Work inside parentheses first. Next evaluate powers, then multiplication and division from left to right, and finally addition and subtraction from left to right. Operations at the same priority are evaluated in their written order; multiplication does not always come before division.

## Worked example

A reading circle orders 4 packs of notebooks at ₱35 per pack and pays a ₱20 delivery fee. The total cost is **4 × 35 + 20 = ₱160**. Multiplication gives the notebook cost (₱140); addition includes delivery.

As a check, add 35 + 35 + 35 + 35 + 20. The result is again ₱160.

## Practice

A study group has 8 learners. Each brings 3 practice sheets, and the facilitator brings 6 more. Write an expression for the total number of sheets, then solve it on paper.

## Rationale

The expression is **8 × 3 + 6**. The learners bring 24 sheets, and 6 more brings the total to **30 sheets**. The units stay as sheets throughout. Adding 3 and 6 before multiplying would incorrectly treat the facilitator's sheets as a contribution from every learner.

## Reflection

Write your own situation with a multiplication step and an addition step. Label the units and check your answer by repeated addition.
