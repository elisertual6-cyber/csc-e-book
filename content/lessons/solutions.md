# Use the key to reconstruct a solution

Draft editorial sample · Common track · Version 0.1

This original, ungraded teaching draft awaits independent correctness, originality, source and language review. It is not an official CSC lesson or a claim of verified 2027 coverage. Check the reasoning behind a proposed answer rather than memorising letters.

## Learning objectives

- Match a key to the exact question identifier and version.
- Reconstruct a numerical or verbal solution independently.
- Identify an ambiguous item and prepare a specific review note.

## Explanation

An answer letter has meaning only within a particular question and choice order. Use the stable item identifier and version when discussing a correction. If options are reordered, a memorised letter may no longer identify the same text. This resource supplies a proposed answer, answer text and explanation for every new question; those are authoring outputs pending independent review, not proof that a question is ready for a scored examination.

For numerical items, translate the prompt into an equation with units, calculate without looking at the key, and use a second check such as substitution, a total or a reasonable bound. For language and reading items, identify the wording that supports the answer. For ordering and logic, test every stated dependency or try a counterexample. A confident explanation should also explain why a plausible distractor fails.

For source-based items, locate the specified article or section and distinguish a paraphrase from an unsupported case-specific claim. Record a concrete issue if more than one option appears defensible. Changing a proposed answer in a file does not count as independent review or retroactively change a learner’s already-created attempt snapshot.

## Worked example

An invented ticket item gives twelve tickets at 50 pesos and eight at 80 pesos. The average is (12 × 50 + 8 × 80)/20 = 62 pesos. A proposed answer of 65 averages the two prices equally while ignoring different quantities. The repair note should identify the weighting error, not simply demand a different letter.

## Practice

1. What information should accompany a report of an incorrect key?
2. Give a second check for a claimed solution x = 12 to the equation x + 3x = 48.
3. What should an author do if two reading options are both fully supported?

## Rationale

For 1, provide the item identifier/version, proposed correction and reasoning without exposing a private live assessment. For 2, substitute: 12 + 36 = 48. For 3, revise the prompt or options and obtain fresh independent review; selecting one arbitrarily does not remove ambiguity.

## Reflection

Use explanations as a starting point for checking. Preserve the distinction between an authored draft key and a genuinely reviewed, released assessment.
