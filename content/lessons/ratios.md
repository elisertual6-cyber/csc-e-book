# Keep the relationship in view

Draft editorial lesson · Common curriculum · Version 0.1

Original teaching material awaiting independent review. The examples are ungraded and do not claim official CSC coverage, scores or timing.

## Learning objectives

- Interpret ratios in their stated order and distinguish part-to-part from part-to-whole comparisons.
- Solve direct proportion problems when the constant-rate assumption is justified.
- Compute a combined mean from totals and group sizes.

## Explanation

The ratio 2:3 describes a relationship: two equal ratio parts of the first quantity for every three of the second. It does not necessarily mean there are only five objects. If the quantities are red and blue folders, in that order, red:blue = 2:3 and red:total = 2:5. Reversing the labels reverses the ratio.

Ratios can be scaled by multiplying both terms by the same positive number. With a total, add the ratio terms to find the number of equal parts. Divide the total by that number, then multiply by each term. When counting indivisible objects, check that the result is a whole number and fits the context.

Two quantities are directly proportional when their quotient stays constant. If one notebook costs ₱18 and there are no discounts or added fees, five cost 5 × ₱18. Do not assume direct proportion when there is a fixed delivery charge, a tiered price or a changing rate.

The arithmetic mean is **total ÷ number of observations**. For two groups, add the totals and divide by the combined count. Averaging the two group means is valid only when the group sizes are equal. A larger group contributes more observations and therefore carries more weight.

## Worked example

A workshop prepares 45 folders. The requested ratio of blue to green folders is 2:3.

1. There are 2 + 3 = **5 equal ratio parts**.
2. Each part represents 45 ÷ 5 = **9 folders**.
3. Blue: 2 × 9 = **18 folders**. Green: 3 × 9 = **27 folders**.

Check both conditions: 18 + 27 = 45, and 18:27 simplifies to 2:3. Reporting 2/3 of the total as blue would confuse a part-to-part ratio with a part-to-whole fraction.

## Practice

Show the total and the count or ratio parts before calculating.

1. Divide 56 blank cards between two groups in the ratio 3:4. How many cards does each group receive?
2. A group of 3 learners reads an average of 12 pages each. A second group of 2 learners reads an average of 17 pages each. Find the average across all 5 learners.
3. Six identical pens cost ₱90 with no extra charges. At the same unit price, how much do 10 pens cost?

## Rationale

1. **24 cards and 32 cards**, in the stated order. Seven ratio parts represent 56 cards, so each part is 8 cards. The shares are 3 × 8 and 4 × 8; their sum is 56 and their ratio simplifies to 3:4.
2. **14 pages per learner.** The first group reads 3 × 12 = 36 pages; the second reads 2 × 17 = 34 pages. The combined mean is (36 + 34)/(3 + 2) = 70/5 = 14. Simply averaging 12 and 17 would ignore the unequal group sizes.
3. **₱150.** The unit price is ₱90/6 = ₱15 per pen. Ten pens cost 10 × ₱15. This conclusion relies on the stated constant unit price.

## Reflection

Label every ratio term before solving. For an average, ask whether each number represents one observation or a whole group. Reconstructing totals often makes the correct denominator clear.
