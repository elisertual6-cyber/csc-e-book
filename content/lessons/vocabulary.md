# Let the sentence guide the meaning

Draft editorial sample · Common curriculum · Version 0.1

This original teaching draft awaits independent correctness, originality and language review. It is not an official CSC question or an approved assessment.

## Learning objectives

- Use definition, example and contrast clues to infer a word's meaning.
- Check an inferred meaning against the whole sentence.
- Distinguish a useful clue from an unsupported personal association.

## Explanation

A word can have several meanings. Start with the meaning supported by its sentence, rather than the first definition you remember. Read the words before and after the unfamiliar term. A definition may restate it; an example may illustrate it; a contrast may show what it does not mean.

In “The schedule is flexible: staff may choose either morning or afternoon,” the second clause explains the intended sense of flexible. Here it means adaptable, not physically bendable. The colon helps introduce the explanation, but punctuation alone never determines the meaning.

Contrast signals include although, unlike and however. They identify a relationship, not a complete answer. After inferring a meaning, substitute it into the sentence and check whether every clue still fits. A familiar word can still be wrong if it contradicts the context. If context supplies too little evidence, acknowledge the uncertainty and consult a dictionary when the task permits it.

## Worked example

“Unlike the detailed first draft, the revised notice was concise and kept only the essential instructions.” What does concise mean here?

The comparison with a detailed draft and the phrase “only the essential instructions” support **brief and clear**. “Incomplete” goes too far: the notice still contains essential instructions. “Careless” is unsupported. “Decorative” has no connection to the stated revision. Substitute “brief and clear” and reread the sentence to confirm the meaning.

## Practice

1. “The arrangement is provisional; it will be replaced when the permanent plan is approved.” Explain provisional in your own words and underline the clue.
2. “The directions were ambiguous, so two readers understood them differently.” Explain ambiguous and identify the evidence.
3. Write two sentences using the word light with different meanings. Give enough context to distinguish them.

## Rationale

In item 1, provisional means temporary in this context. Replacement by a permanent plan supports that interpretation. It does not necessarily mean secret, expensive or compulsory.

In item 2, ambiguous means open to more than one interpretation. The different understandings provide the clue; the sentence does not establish that the directions were long or optional. For item 3, “The bag is light enough to carry” concerns weight, while “The light above the desk is bright” concerns illumination.

## Reflection

Keep a small vocabulary notebook with the original sentence, your proposed meaning and the specific clue. A plausible guess becomes more useful when you can explain its evidence. Examples here teach reading strategies and do not establish official examination coverage.
