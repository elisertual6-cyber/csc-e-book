# Separate the sentence from your assumptions

Draft editorial supplement. Original, unreviewed teaching material with proposed solutions. The short passages below are invented and are not official CSC examination text.

## Learning objectives

- Preserve limits such as only, each, complete and during June.
- Choose an answer supported by the words in a passage.
- Identify the grammatical subject before selecting a verb.

## Explanation

An answer can sound reasonable while claiming more than the passage says. Read an option as a claim: identify who it concerns, what happened, when it happened and any restriction on the group. A passage about complete applications does not automatically describe incomplete applications.

Grammar requires the same attention to structure. The nearest noun is not always the subject. A phrase beginning with “of” can describe the subject without controlling the verb's number.

## Worked example

### Example 1: Keep the restriction in the answer

Read this invented notice: “Desk B received applications on Monday. Only complete applications were checked on Tuesday. Incomplete applications were set aside for follow-up.”

Which claim is supported: “Every application received on Monday was checked on Tuesday” or “Applications checked on Tuesday were complete”?

1. “Only complete applications” restricts the checked group.
2. The notice separately identifies incomplete applications.
3. It does not say that every application received was complete.
4. The supported answer is **Applications checked on Tuesday were complete**.

The first claim removes the restriction and overstates the notice. Do not treat an application's arrival as proof that it met the completeness condition.

### Example 2: Find the subject before checking agreement

Choose the verb: “Each of the files ___ a signed page.” Options: contains / contain.

1. The subject is **Each**.
2. “Of the files” describes the group from which each item is considered.
3. In this construction, each takes a singular verb.
4. The proposed answer is **contains**: “Each of the files contains a signed page.”

The plural noun “files” is nearby, but it is not the subject that determines this verb.

## Practice

Read: “During June, a temporary assistance desk opens on Mondays and Thursdays. Its July schedule will be announced separately.”

Which conclusion is supported?

A. The desk opens every weekday in July.
B. The stated June schedule includes Mondays and Thursdays.
C. The desk never operates in any other month.
D. The July schedule must match June.

Write the exact words that support your choice. Then identify the extra assumption in one rejected option.

## Rationale

The proposed answer is **B — The stated June schedule includes Mondays and Thursdays**. The first sentence directly supports it. The notice leaves July for a separate announcement, so A and D invent that month's schedule. C turns a temporary June statement into a claim about every other month without evidence.

## Common mistake

Do not substitute what an office usually does for what this particular notice states. An inference must remain within the passage's conditions, even when a broader answer feels familiar.
