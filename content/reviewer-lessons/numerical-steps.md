# Keep the base and the units visible

Draft editorial supplement. Original, unreviewed teaching material with proposed solutions. These examples are not official CSC questions or a verified examination blueprint.

## Learning objectives

- Identify the amount on which each percentage operates.
- Convert minutes to hours before finding an hourly rate.
- Check a calculation by reversing the operation and keeping the units.

## Explanation

A percentage is a fraction of a particular base. When the amount changes, the base for the next percentage can change too. Write each intermediate amount instead of combining percentage figures in your head. A discount followed by an increase is a sequence of multiplications.

A rate needs equally careful labels. Kilometres divided by hours gives kilometres per hour; dividing kilometres by minutes gives a different unit. Converting the time after losing its label is a common source of a plausible but incorrect answer.

## Worked example

### Example 1: A discount followed by an increase

An item costs 800 units. A store reduces the price by 20%, then later increases the reduced price by 25%. What is the final price?

1. The discount is 20% of 800, or 160.
2. The reduced price is 800 − 160 = 640.
3. The increase is 25% of **640**, or 160.
4. The final price is 640 + 160 = **800 units**.

Check: 800 × 0.80 × 1.25 = 800. The price returns to the original because the multipliers have product 1, not because the percentage figures are equal. A 20% increase on 640 would produce only 768.

### Example 2: An hourly speed with minutes in the question

A vehicle travels 54 km in 40 minutes at constant speed. Find its speed in kilometres per hour.

1. Convert the time: 40 ÷ 60 = 2/3 hour.
2. Divide distance by time: 54 ÷ (2/3) = 54 × 3/2.
3. The speed is **81 km/h**.

Check: 81 km/h × 2/3 hour = 54 km. The hours cancel. The value 54 ÷ 40 = 1.35 has units of kilometres per minute, so it is not the requested hourly answer.

## Practice

A service has a listed price of 1,500 units. A hypothetical offer takes 12% off that price, then adds a 5% handling charge calculated on the discounted amount. Find the amount due. These are invented commercial terms, not a statement about taxes or required fees.

Before reading the rationale, label the base for each percentage and estimate whether the final amount should be above or below 1,500.

## Rationale

The discount is 1,500 × 0.12 = 180, leaving 1,320. The handling charge is 1,320 × 0.05 = 66. The proposed answer is **1,386 units**. Equivalently, 1,500 × 0.88 × 1.05 = 1,386. Adding 5% of the original amount would use the wrong base.

## Common mistake

Do not subtract 12 and add 5 to call the total change a 7% decrease. The two percentages apply to different amounts. Keep the intermediate value even when a mental estimate seems obvious.
