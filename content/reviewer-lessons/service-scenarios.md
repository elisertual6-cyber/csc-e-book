# Apply the stated rule and limit the claim

Draft editorial supplement. Original, unreviewed teaching material with proposed solutions. The rules below are invented training instructions, not quotations from law or advice about an actual legal case.

## Learning objectives

- Connect a public-service choice with a stated fairness rule.
- Explain what a resource comparison supports and what it leaves unknown.
- Preserve privacy requirements when following a complaint-handling scenario.

## Explanation

A scenario usually supplies both an objective and a constraint. A useful answer must satisfy the objective without quietly removing the constraint. Helping a client quickly, for example, does not by itself justify ignoring the exercise's stated queue rule.

Claims about environmental outcomes also need limits. Measuring fewer sheets of paper does not measure every environmental effect of an activity. State the observed quantity and avoid converting one comparison into a universal guarantee.

## Worked example

### Example 1: Apply the queue rule consistently

Training rule: “Accept routine applications in queue order. An urgent exception needs a documented reason.” A staff member's relative requests priority for a routine application without giving an urgent reason. What response fits the rule?

1. The application is routine under the facts provided.
2. No reason meeting the exception has been documented.
3. Being related to a staff member is not an exception in this training rule.
4. The proposed answer is **Keep the application in the ordinary queue and explain the same procedure used for other clients**.

The exercise illustrates consistency and fairness. It does not establish a legal finding about an actual employee or agency.

### Example 2: Keep an environmental claim tied to the measure

An invented office records 500 sheets for one comparable task before changing its process. It then records 350 sheets for the same-sized task. What change is supported?

1. The reduction is 500 − 350 = 150 sheets.
2. The reduction relative to the original amount is 150 ÷ 500 = 30%.
3. The supported statement is **Recorded paper use for that comparable task fell by 30%**.
4. A claim of “zero environmental impact” is not supported: energy, transport and other effects were not measured here.

Check that the task sizes and counting methods are comparable before applying the percentage to a real situation.

## Practice

Training rule: “Log complaints securely, provide a reference number, and omit personal identifiers from public summaries.” A client submits a complaint. Which response follows all three instructions?

A. Publish the full complaint with the client's identifying details.
B. Discard the complaint after giving a verbal promise.
C. Record it securely, provide its reference number, and use an anonymized public summary if a summary is needed.
D. Announce the client's name before deciding whether to log it.

Identify the instruction that each rejected option fails to preserve.

## Rationale

The proposed answer is **C — Record it securely, provide its reference number, and use an anonymized public summary if a summary is needed**. It preserves secure logging, a reference the client can use and the restriction on public identifiers. A and D remove the privacy constraint. B omits the required record and reference. In an actual service, follow the applicable law and agency procedures; the invented rule is only the basis of this exercise.

## Common mistake

Do not let a positive-sounding goal such as transparency or speed erase an explicit condition. Explain which facts and rules support your conclusion, and do not claim a broader legal or environmental result without evidence.
