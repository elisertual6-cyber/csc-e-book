# Turn a result into your next study session

Draft editorial supplement. Original, unreviewed teaching material with proposed solutions. Schedules and scores below are learning examples, not official preparation requirements or passing predictions.

## Learning objectives

- Turn a limited amount of study time into a specific next action.
- Use an error log to identify a method to revisit.
- Reconstruct a proposed solution and check it with an independent operation.

## Explanation

A score describes one attempt. To make it useful, look at the questions you missed or left unanswered and name the step that caused difficulty. “Review mathematics” is broad; “convert minutes to hours before calculating a speed” gives a concrete target.

Keep a short error log with the question's topic, your attempted method, the point where it failed and one corrected example. Then try another example without looking at the solution. A completed plan or a high practice score does not guarantee readiness for a future examination.

## Worked example

### Example 1: Give a short session a clear structure

A learner has 30 minutes and repeatedly forgets to identify the base in percentage questions. Plan one focused session.

1. Spend 5 minutes recalling the percentage method without notes.
2. Spend 10 minutes following and explaining one worked example.
3. Spend 10 minutes trying new examples without viewing the solutions.
4. Spend 5 minutes checking the work and recording the main error.

The proposed plan is **5 + 10 + 10 + 5 = 30 minutes**. Choose a smaller target if the examples take longer. This is an adjustable routine, not a required allocation of examination time.

### Example 2: Reconstruct an answer rather than memorising a letter

An exercise combines ten scores with mean 60 and thirty scores with mean 90. A learner averages the two means and proposes 75. Check that reasoning.

1. The first group's total is 10 × 60 = 600.
2. The second group's total is 30 × 90 = 2,700.
3. The combined mean is (600 + 2,700) ÷ 40 = **82.5**.
4. Check: 82.5 × 40 = 3,300, the same combined total.

The simple average 75 gives the two groups equal weight even though one has three times as many scores. A useful error-log entry is “Use group sizes when combining means,” not “Remember option B.”

## Practice

You have only 15 minutes. Design a short session for one recently missed skill using 5 minutes of recall, 7 minutes of fresh practice and 3 minutes of checking. Write what you will recall, what you will try and what you will put in the error log.

Then decide when you can revisit that skill. Choose a realistic day rather than treating a missed study day as a failed plan.

## Rationale

The proposed structure fits because **5 + 7 + 3 = 15 minutes**. A useful answer names a single skill, such as comparing completion percentages with the correct denominators, and includes a fresh example and an explicit check. There is no single required calendar or guaranteed score improvement. Adapt the next session using what the check actually shows.

## Common mistake

Do not turn every wrong answer into a need to reread an entire subject. Identify the missing step, practise it deliberately and check whether you can explain it with different numbers or a new passage.
